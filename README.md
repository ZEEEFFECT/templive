. Vision

To create a culture where security is everyone’s responsibility — woven into every decision, project, and interaction — enabling trust, innovation, and operational excellence.

Expanded Explanation:

Our goal is not only to protect systems and data, but to embed security thinking into the company’s DNA.
This vision positions security as a business enabler that empowers innovation rather than restricting it. Every employee — from leadership to interns — should see their role in protecting the organization as natural and meaningful.

Example in Practice:

Product managers include security in early design discussions.

Engineers automatically consider least-privilege principles when creating new services.

Non-technical staff recognize phishing attempts and confidently report them.

KPIs / Success Indicators:

≥ 90 % of employees complete mandatory security awareness training.

≥ 70 % participation rate in voluntary “Security Awareness Days” or phishing simulations.

≥ 30 % increase in proactive incident reports from non-security teams.

2. Expected Behavior We Want to Promote

We want employees to act securely by default — not because they must, but because they understand why it matters.

Desired Behaviors:

Proactive Thinking: Employees question unusual requests, double-check links, and verify identities.

Transparency: Mistakes or near-misses are reported immediately, without fear of blame.

Collaboration: Security is discussed in team stand-ups, project reviews, and post-mortems.

Ownership: Each team defines how security applies to its processes and tracks improvements.

Data Responsibility: All staff treat customer and company data as confidential assets.

Continuous Learning: Teams stay up-to-date through micro-training and peer-sharing.

Example in Practice:

Developers run automated security scans before committing code.

Finance staff verify supplier bank details via a second channel before payment.

Managers reinforce secure behaviors during 1:1 meetings.

KPIs:

Reduction in click-through rate during simulated phishing campaigns.

Measurable increase in incident reporting and collaboration tickets.

Employee surveys reflect higher “I feel responsible for security” responses.

3. The Single Attribute Security Should Stand For

“Trust through Accountability.”

Security should represent reliability, transparency, and ownership.
We want employees and customers alike to associate our organization with integrity in digital operations.

Why It Matters:

A strong cultural attribute aligns everyone toward a shared identity.
When security is seen as accountable and enabling, trust follows — internally (between teams) and externally (with partners and clients).

Example in Practice:

Leaders openly discuss incidents and lessons learned (“we own and fix our mistakes”).

Security dashboards are transparent — not hidden behind IT jargon.

Every employee understands how their role ties into data protection.

KPIs:

Improved internal trust scores in culture surveys (“I trust the company to handle data securely”).

Fewer audit findings related to unclear ownership.

Increased engagement in security-champion programs.

4. What We Want to Get Rid Of

To transform culture, we must actively eliminate outdated mindsets and unproductive perceptions.

Behaviors / Perceptions to Eliminate:
Outdated Mindset	New Mindset
“Security slows us down.”	“Security enables us to move safely and faster.”
“It’s IT’s job.”	“It’s everyone’s job.”
“Mistakes get punished.”	“We learn from mistakes.”
“Compliance = security.”	“Risk awareness + accountability = security.”
“Security is boring.”	“Security is part of innovation and trust.”
Example in Practice:

Replace blame-oriented post-incident reviews with “learning sessions.”

Highlight positive examples in company newsletters.

Eliminate bureaucracy that blocks secure productivity (e.g., simplify access requests).

KPIs:

Employee survey: reduction in “security is a blocker” perception by 30 %.

Increased voluntary reporting after near-misses.

Shorter average time to resolve reported security issues.

5. What We Will Be Proud Of if Successful

“When security becomes invisible — because it’s naturally embedded into how we work.”

Indicators of Success:

Employees instinctively apply secure practices without reminders.

Security metrics show continuous improvement in awareness, compliance, and incident response.

Other departments request security collaboration early in projects.

Customers cite trust and reliability as reasons for doing business with us.

External audits confirm cultural maturity, not just technical compliance.

Example in Practice:

Internal recognition: Security Culture Champion of the Year award.

Zero unreported phishing or DLP events within 12 months.

Consistent integration of security in product roadmaps and sprint planning.
