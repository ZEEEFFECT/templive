Security Culture Vision Framework
1. Vision

To embed security as a natural, shared responsibility across all levels of the company — where secure behavior is not enforced but instinctive, and every employee acts as a proactive defender of the organization’s data, systems, and reputation.

2. What is the Expected Behavior We Want to Promote?

Employees think before they click, share, or approve access.

Teams report incidents, mistakes, and suspicious activity early without fear.

Managers integrate security considerations into daily decisions and project planning.

Developers build with security by design, following secure coding and DevOps practices.

Everyone treats company and customer data as if it were their own.

Security teams collaborate with business units instead of acting as gatekeepers.

✅ Goal: Create a “Security First Mindset” — where security is embedded in every action and decision, not an afterthought.

3. What is the Single Attribute / Remark Security at the Company Should Stand For?

“Trust through Accountability.”

Security should be known internally and externally for reliability, transparency, and ownership.
Every employee should understand: “If I handle information or systems securely, I protect our customers, our reputation, and my colleagues.”

Alternate positioning ideas (depending on your company tone):

“Security = Empowerment, not restriction.”

“Security means we care about trust.”

“We build secure habits, not just secure systems.”

4. What Do We Want to Get Rid Of in Terms of Our Perception at the Company?

The belief that security slows things down or blocks innovation.

The perception that security is only IT’s or compliance’s job.

The culture of fear or silence after mistakes (e.g., not reporting phishing or data mishandling).

The checkbox mentality — focusing on passing audits instead of reducing risk.

Inconsistent leadership communication about why security matters.

✅ We move from control and compliance → to collaboration and ownership.

5. What is the Single Thing We Will Be Proud Of if Successful?

Seeing security integrated into the DNA of every department.

Having employees who proactively report suspicious activity or near-misses.

A visible reduction in incidents caused by human error or policy violations.

Positive audit and regulatory outcomes without last-minute panic.

Security viewed as a business enabler, not a cost center.

✅ Pride Point: When external auditors, partners, and customers say,

“This company truly lives and breathes security.”

6. What is the Time Frame to Establish Everything?
Phase	Time Frame	Objective
Phase 1 – Foundation	0–6 months	Establish leadership buy-in, define key behaviors, create awareness campaigns.
Phase 2 – Integration	6–12 months	Embed security in daily workflows, onboard champions, measure engagement.
Phase 3 – Maturity	12–24 months	Continuous training, KPIs, recognition program, integrate with performance goals.
Phase 4 – Sustain & Evolve	24+ months	Refresh programs, benchmark against peers, maintain innovation-driven culture.
